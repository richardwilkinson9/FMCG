# The pitch kit — for podcasts, press and partners

One page to attach or paste when someone asks "so what is it?"

## The one-liner

GROSS. is a set of free commercial calculators for UK FMCG brand teams —
retailer margins, gross-to-net, trade spend payback, cash flow, Amazon and
TikTok fees — built by a grocery commercial director who got tired of
watching brands lose meetings to arithmetic.

## The three-sentence version

Every week, brand teams walk into range reviews knowing their own margin to
one decimal place and the deal's real shape to none. GROSS. does the maths a
finance team would do if challenger brands had one: what the retailer makes,
what a promo actually costs, what margin survives a bad forecast, and when
the cash actually lands. It is free, needs no sign-up, exports a live Excel
model, and every fee default is dated and sourced on a public rate card.

## Numbers that make segments (all worked examples, all on the site)

1. **Their 35% is not your 35%.** Front margin is measured off the shelf
   price, yours off net revenue. Same word, two numbers; the buyer knows both.
2. **48p.** On one worked listing, the margin per unit that survives a halved
   rate of sale. Margin is not the prize for a good forecast — it is the
   insurance on a bad one. (The Ledger No. 001, working shown.)
3. **Paid in 60, owe in 30.** A healthy listing can need tens of thousands in
   funding before it pays for itself. Brands rarely die of bad margins; they
   die of good margins with long payment terms.
4. **Amazon eats 35–50%** of a low-priced FMCG product before cost of goods —
   and case size does more about it than any negotiation, because there is no
   negotiation.

## Sample questions to ask (for hosts)

- What does a buyer's "we're investing in price" cost the brand, in pence?
- Why do challenger brands agree to margins that can't survive a promo year?
- What's the one number a founder should know before a range review?
- Where does the cash actually go in the first year of a listing?

## The honesty rules (why the audience trusts it)

- Every fee default is dated and sourced on The Rate Card; wrong figures get
  corrected and the correction dated.
- Worked examples are declared as worked examples — no invented market data.
- The calculators and the Excel export are free and stay free. The only ask
  anywhere is an email for a monthly letter.
